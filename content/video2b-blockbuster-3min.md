## The chain

The hook promises something specific — *it built its own Netflix and it worked* — then
withholds the reason it died until the board appears:

| Section | Ends by making you ask… |
|---|---|
| The offer | *"who killed the thing that was working?"* |
| The giant | nine years left to live — how? |
| The engine | *"the one thing it couldn't comfortably do…"* |
| It fought back | *"all it needed was permission to lose money."* |
| Then the board | the answer lands here |
| The collapse | …but was it stupidity? |
| The winner stumbled too | no — here's the proof |
| Your late fee | turns on the viewer |

The hook is deliberately adversarial: *"That is the version you have heard. Here is the
version almost nobody tells."* That's a promise of new information, which is what the
9-minute original lacked at the open.
